University Projects

Applying Computer Science to practical problems.

From software and datasets to simulation, optimisation and evidence-based recommendations.

My degree involved software development, data analysis, quantitative work and independent research. Projects required me to work with complex datasets, develop computational solutions, test different approaches and communicate findings clearly.

A major research project investigated the optimal location of volunteer emergency-service resources, combining geographic information, travel-time analysis, optimisation and simulation.

Emergency Services Location Optimisation

The project modelled a real operational problem: where a volunteer emergency response resource should be based when responders travel from home, availability varies and different response strategies can produce different outcomes.

  • Processed real and simulated incident data.
  • Worked with travel-time and geographic information.
  • Developed algorithms to compare potential operational locations.
  • Modelled changing volunteer-availability scenarios.
  • Used simulation to test performance under uncertainty.
  • Compared alternative operational response strategies.
  • Analysed large numbers of potential response scenarios.
  • Translated technical results into practical recommendations.

The work demonstrated how data, programming, quantitative analysis and optimisation can be applied to a real operational decision rather than treated purely as theoretical exercises.
